The competitor that isn't 3D

AI food photography

Same budget, same job, a fifth of the price — and two of the largest food platforms give it away inside tools the restaurant already opens every day.

FoodShot AI$0.36 per image, at the 250-image annual tier
MenuPhotoAI$27 per month entry, credits never expire
DoorDash · Uber EatsFree AI photo enhancement built into the merchant tools
Their claim24–35% more orders with professional photos

Photos cannot show portion size on a real table, cannot report which dishes a guest lingered on, and are drawing criticism precisely because the plate that arrives does not match the picture. Those three gaps are the pitch. “Your food will look better” is not.

What follows from this

  1. Lift the five-dish ceiling with a database flag. Set thumb_3d = false on non-featured items and keep featured_items driving the 3D block. Whole menu listed as photos, five dishes promoted in 3D — the scarcity play gets stronger, not weaker, against forty plain cards.
  2. Rebuild the ladder as 5 / 20 / unlimited. At $22 per dish we sit last on the pricing table, and that only made sense while the ceiling was real.
  3. Copy ARmenu's intake. The venue sends 20–30 photos per dish over WhatsApp, we process, 48 hours back. No travel, no scheduling, no photographer — which is exactly how they afford unlimited at £199.
  4. Lead with model weight. 1.6 MB against 8.2 MB is measurable, demonstrable on a phone in the room, and nobody else in the category can say it.
  5. Retire “first in Georgia.” Literally true, but JARIT is one country over and Menu AR franchises territories with Georgia unclaimed. Both are one search away.
